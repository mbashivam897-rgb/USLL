**Key observations**

1. **The four trading multiples cluster tightly (₹1,316–₹1,440, mean ≈ ₹1,400).** This convergence is itself evidence: it shows that, irrespective of which line of the P&L an investor anchors to (sales, EBITDA, EBIT or net profit), a justified peer-and-history-based multiple lands USL fair value in the ~₹1,300–1,450 band, broadly in line with the current price. Relative valuation is internally consistent for USL.

2. **The DCF (₹743) is the clear outlier — and it is a *downside* outlier**, ~47% below the multiples cluster and ~41% below the market price. This is **not** a data error; it is a structural feature of USL's economics and is corroborated by the model's own commentary. Two forces depress the intrinsic value:
   - **High working-capital intensity** (cash-conversion cycle ~50–63 days; inventory held 130–145 days for whisky maturation; receivables ~45–48 days tied to slow-paying state beverage corporations). This caps free-cash-flow conversion well below reported EBITDA/PAT.
   - **The market is implicitly capitalising a far longer premiumisation runway** — a sub-10% discount rate and >7% perpetual growth — than a disciplined two-stage DCF will credit. The DCF therefore behaves as an **intrinsic-value floor / margin-of-safety anchor**, not as the primary lens.

3. **Most relevant methodologies for USL:** **EV/EBITDA** and **P/E**. USL is a mature, highly profitable (EBITDA margin ~18%, RoCE ~26%), cash-generative, net-cash market leader; the Indian market prices such consumer-staples/spirits franchises primarily on forward earnings and EBITDA multiples.

4. **Lower-importance methodology among multiples:** **EV/Revenue.** It ignores the very thing that defines the USL investment case — *profitability and mix*. A pure sales multiple cannot distinguish USL's ~18–20% EBITDA margin from a 10%-margin bulk-alcohol peer, so it is the least informative for a premiumisation/margin-expansion story.

5. **Outlier treatment:** the **DCF receives reduced (not zero) weight**. It is retained because it is the only methodology grounded in intrinsic cash generation and it disciplines against over-paying; but its weight is capped so that a single conservative cash-flow read does not dominate a valuation that the market, peers and USL's own history all price materially higher.

## B. Weightages and Their Justification

We deliberately **reject equal weighting**. Weights are set by (i) USL's business characteristics, (ii) spirits-industry valuation convention, (iii) profitability/maturity, (iv) reliability of the cash-flow forecast, and (v) earnings- vs revenue-multiple relevance.

| Methodology | Weight | Rationale |
|---|--:|---|
| **EV/EBITDA** | **28%** | Industry-standard for spirits/alcobev; capital-structure- and D&A-neutral; cleanest cross-peer comparison. Highest single weight. |
| **P/E** | **22%** | The metric Indian equity investors anchor to for consumer staples; captures full capital structure, tax and below-EBIT items; reflects USL's net-cash, low-tax-leakage quality. |
| **DCF (FCFF)** | **30%** | Theoretically the most rigorous (intrinsic value), but an outlier here due to WC intensity and conservative terminal assumptions. Material weight as the margin-of-safety anchor, **capped below a simple 50/50** so it does not override the market-validated relative cluster. |
| **EV/EBIT** | **12%** | Consistency cross-check on operating profitability that also charges for D&A. Because USL is asset-light (D&A ~2% of NSV), EV/EBIT ≈ EV/EBITDA, so it is partly **redundant** with method 1 → moderate weight to avoid double-counting. |
| **EV/Revenue** | **8%** | Included only as a sanity check and because it is the most robust to one-off margin distortions. Lowest weight: it ignores profitability and mix, which are the core drivers of USL's value. |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

**Why each is *included*:** EV/EBITDA and P/E are the two lenses the spirits/staples market actually uses, so they must carry the majority of the relative weight. EV/EBIT validates that the EBITDA-based answer survives a depreciation charge. EV/Revenue guards against the relative case resting entirely on margin assumptions. DCF ensures the price is defensible on intrinsic cash flows, not just on peer sentiment.

**Why the alternatives receive *lower* weight:**
- **vs EV/Revenue (8%):** sales multiples are appropriate for early-stage, loss-making or pre-margin businesses — the opposite of USL, a profit-mature leader. Hence the smallest weight.
- **vs EV/EBIT (12%):** for an asset-light company it adds little incremental information beyond EV/EBITDA; weighting it heavily would effectively double-count the EBITDA view.
- **vs DCF (30% rather than ~40–50%):** the forecast's reliability is real but bounded — FCFF is sensitive to the WC assumption and to terminal growth, and the output diverges sharply from both peers and USL's own multi-year trading range. A reduced weight respects the methodology while acknowledging the outlier.

**Net stance:** relative methods carry **70%** (the market prices USL on multiples); DCF carries **30%** (intrinsic discipline). This blend is the convention among sell-side analysts covering Indian consumer staples.

## C. Selection of Appropriate Trading Multiples

| Multiple | Most common in spirits? | Captures operating performance? | Investor focus | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EV/EBITDA** | **Yes — the spirits-industry default** | Strong | High (institutional / M&A) | Capital-structure & tax neutral; removes D&A and depreciation-policy noise; best cross-peer comparability; the multiple used in alcobev M&A | Ignores capex/WC intensity (a real USL drag); flatters cash conversion |
| **P/E** | Common | Moderate (post-tax, post-interest) | **Highest among retail & long-only equity investors** | Simple, widely quoted; reflects full capital structure, tax efficiency and net-cash interest income | Distorted by one-offs/exceptionals (e.g., USL's FY23 brand-sale gain, FY26 discontinued-ops gain) and by leverage/tax differences across peers |
| **EV/EBIT** | Less common | **Strongest pure-operating read** (charges D&A) | Moderate | Penalises capital intensity that EBITDA hides; good for asset-light vs asset-heavy comparison | For asset-light USL it nearly equals EV/EBITDA, so adds limited new information |
| **EV/Revenue** | Rare for profitable spirits names | Weak (ignores margin) | Low | Robust to margin one-offs; usable when earnings are volatile/negative | Blind to profitability and mix — the heart of the USL premiumisation thesis; least suitable here |

**Conclusions:**
- **Most commonly used in spirits/alcobev:** **EV/EBITDA** — it is the sector default and the basis for industry transactions.
- **Best captures operating performance:** **EV/EBIT** in principle (it charges for D&A), but for USL's asset-light model EV/EBITDA does so almost as well and is more comparable across peers.
- **What investors focus on most:** **P/E** for the broad equity market; **EV/EBITDA** for institutional and strategic investors. USL's quality (net cash, ~26% RoCE, low tax leakage) shows up favourably in *both*.
- **Least suitable:** **EV/Revenue**, because it cannot reward the margin expansion that is USL's central equity story.

## E. Investment Recommendation

**Rating: HOLD (Neutral). Target Price ₹1,215 (−3.5% vs CMP ₹1,259).**

**Justification.** USL is the highest-quality, market-leading proxy for India's structural spirits-premiumisation theme, with a fortress balance sheet (net cash ~₹2,721 cr, ~26% RoCE), an asset-light model (~14x asset turnover) and a multi-year margin-expansion runway (EBITDA margin 18.3% → ~20.5%). The relative-valuation cluster (₹1,316–₹1,440) confirms the market is pricing this quality rationally rather than excessively. However, the intrinsic DCF (₹743) is a stark reminder that, on disciplined cash-flow assumptions, the stock already discounts years of double-digit growth. The blended fair value of **₹1,215** therefore sits just below the market — quality is "in the price."

**Key valuation drivers:**
- **Premiumisation:** P&A now ~90% of NSV; realisation compounding ~5%+ p.a. — the engine behind EBITDA-margin and EPS growth.
- **Margin expansion:** mix, operating leverage and the supply-agility productivity programme.
- **Capital quality:** net cash + ~60% payout + high RoCE support a premium multiple to mass-market peers (ABDL, Globus) and only a modest discount to faster-growing Radico Khaitan.
- **Optionality:** RCB / "Sports" monetisation (held-for-sale), conservatively valued at ₹2,500 cr — upside if crystallised above this mark.

**Key risks to the valuation:**
- **State taxation/regulation:** excise hikes not passed through, route-to-market disruption (Delhi/Andhra precedents) or prohibition.
- **Pricing lag:** state-set MRPs trail input inflation (ENA/grain, scotch concentrate, glass, packaging), capping margin-recovery speed.
- **Working-capital intensity:** the principal reason the DCF lags the multiples — any further CCC deterioration directly erodes intrinsic value.
- **Competition:** Pernod Ricard, Radico Khaitan, ABDL and a post-Imperial-Blue Tilaknagar intensifying premium competition.
- **Multiple de-rating:** at ~30x forward EV/EBITDA and ~44x forward P/E, disappointment on growth or margins carries asymmetric downside.

**Action:** *Hold* core positions for the compounding and RCB optionality. **Accumulate below ~₹1,050–1,100**, where the blended fair value turns positive and a margin of safety opens up; **trim above ~₹1,450**. Catalysts to watch: RCB sale crystallisation, state pricing actions, P&A volume momentum and the input-cost trajectory.

## F. Methodology Notes (reproducibility)

- Implied prices are computed in `final_target_price.py` (imports the same `usl_engine`/`usl_build` used for the report's DCF and comps), so every figure ties back to the live model.
- **EV-based methods:** Implied Equity Value = (Forward metric × Target multiple) + FY26 net cash (₹2,721 cr) + RCB optionality (₹2,500 cr); ÷ 72.78 cr shares.
- **P/E method:** Implied price = Target P/E × FY28E EPS (₹28.4).
- **Target multiples** are anchored to (a) the peer set — Radico Khaitan, Allied Blenders, Tilaknagar, Globus Spirits — excluding low-margin Globus as a distorting outlier, and (b) USL's own de-rated forward multiples (FY28E at CMP: EV/EBITDA 30.4x, EV/EBIT 34.3x, P/E 44.3x, EV/Sales 5.9x), with a justified premium for leadership, mix and returns.
- **DCF** is the report's base case (2-stage FCFF; explicit FY27–30 + premiumisation fade FY31–36; WACC 10.25%, terminal g 6.25%).

*This analysis defends every weighting decision with reasoning; it does not merely average the valuation outputs.*
